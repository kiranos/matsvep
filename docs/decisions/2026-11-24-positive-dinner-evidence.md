# Positive dinner evidence and protein compounds

Production 100-recipe samples showed non-dinners among candidates, uncertain vegetarian labels and protein gaps. Untagged recipes now need a main protein or a seeded dinner dish word. A carb base alone cannot make a side dish a dinner. Seeded preparation, condiment and drink title terms exclude a recipe only when found in its leading dish name, and only when that part has no protein or dinner-dish evidence. This protects dinners with stock or dough in an accompaniment.

Sweet pies are excluded using seeded sweet title and ingredient terms or a configurable sugar-line share. Foreign accents are folded in feature term matching, but Swedish `å`, `ä` and `ö` are never folded. The new `non_dinner_title` reason distinguishes leading-title exclusions from breakfast and side exclusions.

Pilot review exposed two substring collisions: a condiment term inside a meat-dish title, and a dessert foam term inside a sauce method adjective. These terms now require a word ending. Citrus is a sweet-pie signal only when named in the title; a lemon used to season a fish pie is not dessert evidence. Seeded frittata and pasta-shape titles preserve untagged dinners that lack a qualifying main protein.

The protein matcher previously chose the shorter egg suffix in a pork compound. A vegetarian prefix still wins a meat suffix, while a meat prefix now wins a shorter vegetarian suffix; an explicit egg compound can still win. Additional stems and vegetarian protein seed rows address the observed coverage gaps. Specific vegetable and mushroom stocks are seeded as vegetarian so the generic stock stem does not mark them as animal evidence. A substantial vegetarian protein item supplies the main-protein code even when an unrelated unmatched ingredient keeps vegetarian status unknown. Manual feature overrides still apply after derivation. Migration 024 adds the sweet-pie threshold and expands the exclusion-reason check without editing applied migrations.
